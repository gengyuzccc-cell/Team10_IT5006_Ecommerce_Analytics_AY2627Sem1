\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{The models are fitted on a moving target, and the largest driver of
lateness is not observable.} Mean lead time falls 5.34 days between training and
test, so a model fitted on the slower past is off-level on the faster present ---
the positive bias in every region of Section~\ref{sec:results-segments} --- and
needs refitting, with its calibration, on a rolling basis rather than once
\citep{gama2014drift}. The late rate swings between 1.4\% and 21.4\% month to
month with a Black Friday surge, a carrier-side slowdown under a tightened
promise and a national strike (Section~\ref{sec:eda-lateness}): shocks to carrier
capacity or to the platform's own promising, none an attribute of an order. This,
not the choice of algorithm, is the binding constraint on Problem 1B, which is
why the ceiling was stated in advance.

\paragraph{The benefit of a contact is assumed, not measured.}
Section~\ref{sec:cost} values a contact at the low rating it turns around, and
the tables record no contact with customers other than the survey, so whether
reaching a customer earlier changes the rating --- or whether an unsolicited
contact lowers it --- is unobserved. Part of it can be checked --- whether the
customer had already written --- so each contact is counted only on the
customers it can still reach (Appendix~\ref{app:timing}).
The counts of Table~\ref{tab:customer} stand on their own; the reais do not, and
read as the value \emph{if} the pilot of Section~\ref{sec:pilot} finds the
effect. Until it runs, the recommendation is to pilot, not to scale.

\paragraph{Risks to the customers the recommendations serve.}
A margin lengthens the average quoted wait by 1.1 days and the dates it still
misses are missed by more, so how long to quote stays a commercial decision. A
warning shown to almost half of all buyers would stop meaning anything, which is
why it is confined to the riskiest tenth. Three in four customers on the
delivery-day list did not go on to rate the order 1--2 stars, so a contact must
be an offer of help that is easy to decline. Asking for the review once the order is complete
changes when a customer is asked, never whether, and the pilot measures its effect
separately from the message's. And the forecast is least accurate where deliveries
are longest (MAE 6.84 days in the North against 3.42 in the Southeast) while the
margin is national, so broken promises should be tracked by region.

\paragraph{What the data does not contain, and what rests on assumption.}
The target of Problem 2 is a platform rating, with the self-selection
Section~\ref{sec:problem} describes: a model of the rating is not a model of
satisfaction, and the 1--2 star label cannot see the quietly dissatisfied
customer who never reviews. The service-quality literature identifies product
expectation gaps, seller communication and returns handling among the drivers of
dissatisfaction \citep{parasuraman2005esqual, rao2011elsq}; no order-level field
records them, so the Problem 2 residual is unobserved cause rather than noise,
which the comments, written with the rating, describe but cannot predict
(Section~\ref{sec:reviews}). The outputs are triage scores, not entitlements:
they must not penalise a seller, price a customer or drive an automated decision
without review, and since the review-risk model largely restates lateness, a
seller penalised by it is often penalised for a carrier's failure. Everything
here is Brazil, 2017--2018, on one marketplace.

\subsection*{Conclusion}

Two problems, at two moments of the customer's order, from one dataset and one
feature pipeline, carried through to a deployed application. The regression
reaches MAE 3.90 days, 60\% below the platform's own promise; quoted with a margin
chosen on validation, its date would have been broken for 2.5\% of delivered
orders instead of 7.4\%; and thresholded against the promise it makes a better
late-order flag (2.89$\times$ the floor) than any classifier trained on the label
(1.24$\times$), so the brief's dual framing is the better design. The
low-review model reaches 3.75$\times$ its floor, and at its own moment its
daily top ten reach 26 future 1--2 star reviewers in 100. The timing of the
review decided more than the choice of model: most customers let down by a late
or missing parcel had, like the customer of Box~1, rated the order before it came, so they are reached on the
promised date without a model, and the model's list serves the customers whose
parcel arrived but disappointed.

Methodologically, cross-validation inside a drifting training period reorders the
candidates, so selection belongs on several recent windows judged by rank, and
weighting the loss for imbalance leaves ranking unchanged while inflating
predicted risk threefold, so the threshold is the lever. The most useful results
are negative or conditional: an informative flag that does not justify an
expensive action, and contacts whose benefit to customers only a
five-to-seven-week pilot can measure.
